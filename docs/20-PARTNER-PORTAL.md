# Partner portal: contract and decision record

## Scope and decision

The live workspace is mounted at `/partner-portal`. The standalone `demo/offline.html` opens it at `#partner-portal` and keeps state only in the browser tab.

The partner workspace applies to every filed, enabled omni-channel partner and its permitted sub-channels; it is not broker- or agency-specific. The existing customer journey remains the source of quotations, recorded sign-offs, risk information, underwriting and issuance. A journey must be attributed to a partner before its underwriting application is created. A signed, current quotation and a completed application are submitted for **one** review: a supervisor, manager or senior manager may approve, return or reject it. An approver must be a different principal from the submitter. A higher level does not need a previous level to have rejected or timed out; this is fallback authority, not three consecutive approvals.

The partner journey issues an **indicative quotation subject to underwriting** from existing filed inputs. The PDF and prepared email say no decision or cover exists, and the initial sign-off produces no buy token. The application is held against underwriting assessment, medical evidence review and decisions until an eligible partner reviewer approves the signed facts. Approval releases that hold and records the route calculated by the existing rules: straight-through / rules, insurer or senior manual review, independent panel assistance, or reinsurance. Medical requirements are assessed only after approval. A manual route requires a human insurer underwriter independent of the partner submitter and reviewer to review evidence and decide, plus a review bound to that decision. The underwriter must attest that every final condition is represented; terms the engine cannot represent require insurer resolution outside this prototype. Once evidence is reviewed, a changed route is recorded in the audit; a case initially routed to a human remains human-reviewed even if the later route would otherwise be automatic. Declined, postponed and referred cases cannot proceed to payment or cover. See [the conditional quotation decision record](21-CONDITIONAL-QUOTATION.md).

The original partner review is bound to the signed conditional PDF and risk facts. Payment and conversion compare the accepting underwriting decision with the original quote; changed premium, extra mortality or exclusions require a separate revised PDF, bound to the current decision, and a new recorded customer acceptance before proceeding. The revised quote is priced using the current filed inputs and is refused if it cannot reproduce the underwriting decision; no new actuarial assumption is invented. The insurer prepares the quote, the authorized partner can download it, attest it was reviewed with the customer, and record the same customer's new acceptance, and the original signed PDF is retained. **The prototype records names and generates signature references; it does not verify electronic signatures or prove the customer received the PDF.** The email record is not evidence of delivery. Existing medical, reinsurance, cleared payment and conversion gates continue to apply.

Because the legacy journey does not reliably rebuild its underwriting application after a coverage change, a quoted case cannot be amended here: start a new journey and obtain a fresh signed quotation. Direct unscoped underwriting decision and amendment routes reject partner applications. A returned manual case is held again; an already underwritten, paid, routed or issued application cannot be returned through this path and needs insurer cancellation or correction.

A partner may reconcile an insurer-entered commission accrual against its statement, request clarification, and approve a matching figure. Accruals use the existing filed partner/product commission schedule and exact minor-unit currency arithmetic. Settlement entries are **references to externally performed disbursements**; no endpoint sends money, posts a GL journal, or settles a claim. Policy information, financial and non-financial endorsements, claims, settlement and reporting are communication requests, not changes to the underlying policy or claim. The insurer remains responsible for performing the actual downstream work. Commission totals and operational reports are scoped to the partner and, when selected, the sub-channel; money totals are grouped by currency. Statement and external disbursement references are visible with their histories.

## Identity and boundary

The HTTP server derives the actor and roles from its bearer-token principal, not from request fields or the UI role selector. A partner token has `partner:<PARTNER-ID>` plus one or more of `partner-agent`, `partner-supervisor`, `partner-manager`, `partner-senior-manager` or `partner-viewer`. Optional `subchannel:<CODE>` claims narrow access further. Partner principals may only use partner-portal endpoints and health, and partner-scoped requests are checked server-side against the filed channel, partner and sub-channel. A partner may link an existing unattributed journey only when its creator is the authenticated principal; an insurer can attribute one on the partner's behalf. Medical and underwriting notes are restricted to insurer underwriters or administrators, while partner users see only the review status. Internal work needs `admin`, `partner-insurer`, `partner-underwriter`, or `partner-finance`, according to the operation. Demo/offline mode is openly unauthenticated; actor names there are simulations.

## Alternatives considered

- Three sequential approvals: rejected because the request calls for any available authorized level to decide, not all three.
- A separate parallel quote/policy engine: rejected because it would drift from existing signature, underwriting and payment gates.
- Partner-provided commission totals as the source of truth: rejected; the recorded expectation is derived from filed schedules and compared to a separately supplied insurer statement.
- Automatic payout or direct endorsement/claim mutation: rejected because there is no bank integration, durable transactional store or named finance/claims authority in this demo.

## Contract and limitations

The `/api/partner-portal/*` routes share the existing in-memory runtime and `/api` transport rules. Every row and audit event disappears on restart/reset; the reporting-store replay does **not** include this workspace. The portal is a working prototype, not a production multi-tenant boundary, durable queue, external notification system, bank integration, claims settlement service or regulatory approval. Production use requires durable transactions/outbox and immutable audit, verified identity/provisioning and tenant isolation, independently reviewed authority and financial controls, secure document storage/consent, insurer and banking integrations, and named human approval for money-related controls under `AGENTS.md`. Do not treat a reference record as proof that external work or payment occurred. The outstanding reviews and production controls are tracked in the [production-readiness roadmap](22-PARTNER-PRODUCTION-ROADMAP.md).

## Verification

The endpoint and payload contract is published as [`partner-portal.openapi.json`](partner-portal.openapi.json). A contract test checks it against the registered route catalogue and validates representative request/response shapes.

See `src/core/partner-portal.test.ts` and `src/server/partner-portal.test.ts` for workflow and HTTP authorization evidence. After building the UI and server and installing Playwright Chromium, `npm run test:e2e:partner` checks login, sub-channel selection, editor, review, commission, requests, reporting, mobile navigation and the standalone offline workspace in a browser. The UI review/commission rows are test fixtures; the domain test exercises a real signed-and-issued journey. These are automated checks, not an independent verifier or named human approval for live financial operations.
